For the moment you lay out the fabric

Notch lives on the iPad or laptop that's mirrored to your projector, and it turns the pattern PDF you already bought into lines of light on the mat, the right size and the right way up, so you can lay the fabric down, smooth it out and cut along them.

It was made by a sewist who wanted something calmer and better looking than the tools she had, so it keeps the controls out of the way while you work, remembers each pattern exactly where you left it, and hides everything at the press of a key when it's time to trace.

How a cut goes

  1. 1

    Calibrate

    Match four corners to your mat once, and the grid lands at true size every time after.

  2. 2

    Open the PDF

    Projector files open as they are, with every size on its own layer to show or hide.

  3. 3

    Put pages together

    Print-at-home A4 tiles are read by their labels and joined into one sheet, line meeting line.

  4. 4

    Mark the pieces

    Tap inside a piece to select it, name it, and project it on its own when you need room.

  5. 5

    Cut and tick

    Turn, flip and mirror on the fold, then tick each piece off so you always know what's left.
